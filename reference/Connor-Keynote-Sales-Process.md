*Compiled from: Kickoff Sales Process Optimization meeting, Connor's Keynote Sales Call Explanation, and the Connor/Regan systems meeting (Ella walkthrough).*

# PART 1 — MACRO: The Sales Process, Inquiry → Keynote → Cash

Connor runs the same pipeline for Josh (Ella) that we're rebuilding for Ben (Louis). Two lanes feed it — **Direct** (website form → HubSpot/CRM) and **Bureau** (agent email → inbox agent scrapes into the table). Everything after intake converges into one flow.

## Stage 1 — Inquiry & Intake
- **Direct lane:** Website inquiry form → auto-added to CRM. Minimum fields captured: company, event date, contact.
- **Bureau lane:** Agent emails the ops inbox. An email agent (Fixer, on Sharon's machine in Josh's setup) mines the inbox — bureau inboxes are "contained," so every inbound is known to be an agent — and writes the record into Airtable.
- **Instant auto-response** goes out ("thanks, back to you within a couple hours") — this passes the ball, buys triage time, and BCCs the team so a human decides: respond directly, or click into the automation flow.
- **Key-agent override:** a short list of top agents (the "Richard Shelp" tier) gets forwarded straight to Ben — that call is always worth taking personally.

## Stage 2 — Qualification & Date Check
- **The 2-minute review** (Ben's tacit framework, now documented): LinkedIn of the sender → position in company → company itself → if tech, Crunchbase (funding rounds, recency, estimated revenue) → gut read on budget.
- **Rule from experience:** never throw an inquiry away on a budget hunch — "just send the rate card." People clear the fee more often than expected, and the ones who don't balk convert.
- **Edge case awareness:** big logo ≠ big budget (the J.P. Morgan internal-committee example: $5K budget inside a firm that books 8 full-fee deals a year). Division/audience matters more than the parent brand.
- **Date check & holds:** holds live on the color-coded calendar. Each hold carries a stage (inquiry vs. qualified) which drives the cash forecast (see Stage 9). Failure mode to engineer out: stale holds creating false availability.

## Stage 3 — Discovery / Pitch Call
Connor's three-step structure (the structure never changes; the content is improvised off what the buyer says):
1. **Learn.** Open with any known context (growth trajectory, event, industry news) to celebrate or check in; if there's nothing, open with the event itself and ask why they're meeting and what success looks like when the audience walks out the door.
2. **Align credibility.** Take live notes; weave the speaker's 3–5 credibility markers through the lens of the buyer's stated pains/goals. Never pre-scripted — always a response to what they just said.
3. **Surface budget on the call.** Frame it as arming them for their committee meeting, state the fee + travel buyout plainly, ask "how does that sound to you?" — **and pause.** Their reaction sorts the deal instantly (sticker shock / fits / discount ask).
- If the buyer volunteers their budget cap first, even better — you have your rebuttal ready before revealing the fee.
- Discovery questions are pre-set via the questionnaire (sent in advance where possible) so the call double-clicks on answers rather than collecting them cold.

## Stage 4 — Proposal & Follow-Up Cadence
- The proposal is typically an email: rate sheet + speaker reel + testimonials + a custom tailoring paragraph.
- **The #1 move (~50% hit rate): book the next call before hanging up.** Anchor to their stated decision timeline ("we decide in 3 weeks, early that week") and lock the follow-up for the end of that same week. If they choose someone else, *they* cancel the call — which surfaces the "no" weeks earlier than chasing would.
- If no call got booked, cadence is: one soft check-in ("have you talked yet?") — used **once** — then escalate to the **hold-release forcing email**: are you still considering Ben; if not, I'll remove the hold. This forces an answer either way.
- Positioning of the post-decision call: low-risk, short (15 min), "one less thing to juggle," cancel-anytime.

## Stage 5 — Close → Contract → Invoice
- Deal marked **Closed-Won** → contract auto-generated (PandaDoc in Josh's stack) with contract-details request (signer name, company address) sent immediately on green light.
- **Human review gate:** ops (Sharon/Liezel) gets notified, reviews the contract (~30–60 sec), clicks send. Contracts are never worth automating past the review click — a botched contract costs more than the 30 seconds saved.
- Client signs → invoice auto-created from contract terms (one simple line item: "Keynote, 60 minutes, $43,000"), reviewed, sent.
- PandaDoc ↔ QuickBooks sync carries the invoice number downstream.

## Stage 6 — Pre-Event Delivery (where the debrief gets sold)
- On green light, the client also receives the **Keynote Welcome Kit**: questionnaire, stakeholder interviews (if applicable), kickoff call, AV/logistics, game-day details, post-event **strategic debrief listed as item #1 of post-event**.
- **Kickoff call flow:** intros → content review (questionnaire done or worked live) → process flow & timeline. Because the debrief is in the kit and hit in the first five minutes, it's *pre-sold* — nimble orgs lock the date on the spot ("two weeks after the event — does Monday look good?").
- A pre-set agenda gets instant compliance: content → logistics → enhancers → next steps.

## Stage 7 — Event Day & In-Keynote Lead Gen
- AV check, logistics alignment, keynote delivery.
- **QR code** during the talk → email with 3 resources + journal purchase link + an "interested in having Ben speak?" checkbox. (Known flaw being fixed: ~75% check the box by reflex; the fix is a drop-down that expands on check — event timeline, event type — to qualify intent and filter accidental clicks.)
- Checkbox clicks get a same-day follow-up (within ~2 hours) from Liezel or Ben — the exact touch to be systematized so it's a templated, meaningful note, not hand-written each time.

## Stage 8 — Post-Event: Debrief, Repeat Bookings & Upsell
- **Strategic debrief** (booked pre-event, 30 min scheduled, 15–20 actual). Josh runs it on ~7–8 of 10 direct deals.
- Two outcomes hunted on every debrief:
  1. **Future engagement** — repeat booking for next year's event, different audience ("leaders this year, managers next year"), workshop add-on. Co-create next year a year ahead.
  2. **Data** — audience survey results, anecdotal resonance, feedback. Always worth it: it feeds the CRM and system intelligence even with nothing to sell.
- **Bureau caveat:** never overstep the agent. Float the debrief; run it only with the agent looped in (some agents churn-and-burn and skip debriefs entirely).
- Journal bulk orders, dropship-to-attendee orders, and Amazon tail sales ride this stage.

## Stage 9 — Cashflow of Sales (Cashflow IQ)
- Scope is deliberately narrow: **money owed, money paid, money on hold.** No expenses, no travel costs — travel is a flat buyout baked into the fee.
- **Weighted hold forecast:** every calendar hold carries stage 1/2/3 →
  - Stage 1 (inquiry): forecast at **25%** of fee
  - Stage 2 (qualified): forecast at **65%** of fee
  - Stage 3 (closed): **100%**
  - Simple formula field: 10 qualified $10K holds = $65K forecast, not $100K.
- **Payment confirmation loop:** QuickBooks invoice number + amount matched against incoming wire/ACH on the business account (Nemtin International, not personal). Human-in-the-loop on the match (Nikki-equivalent role) — a payment is only ever confirmed by a person.
- Deal tracking continues post-close: contract signed? → invoice sent? → second follow-up on invoice? → hit the bank? Payment plans (~15% of coaching clients, e.g. $2K/mo × 4) live here as a documented standard downsell, not ad-hoc.

# PART 2 — MICRO: The System Flow (what the tool has to do at each step)

This is the build map — each stage translated into data objects, triggers, automations, and where the human sits.

## 2.1 Data Model (agreed hierarchy)
- **Event/Deal = highest order of truth.** The thing being sold sits at the top.
- **Contact = second order**, split into two tables: **Bureau Agents** (known list, contact info) and **Direct Buyers** (meeting planners, decision makers). One-to-many: an event links 1–3+ contacts; blank bureau field on a direct deal is fine — granularity over cleverness.
- Separate but linked domains: **Event data**, **Individual/contact data**, **Journal orders** (and optionally brand deals as a light fourth). Each can function independently — if one breaks, the rest keep working.
- One wide record per event (~40+ fields), tamed with **views** (questionnaire view, logistics view, exec view). Multi-day events = one row per day (Remax #1 / Remax #2 pattern), same process run per row.
- **Last Modified field on every record** — this is the engine for staleness alerts.
- **Backups:** nightly snapshot to Google Sheets (+ a versioned drive) so a bad actor or bad sync can't kill history — "turn back time" recovery. Liezel's existing sheets remain a living fallback; when a step lives on "calendar + sheet," the system mirrors it rather than replaces it.

## 2.2 Flow, step by step

**INQUIRY IN**
1. Direct: website form → record created in Deals table (company, date, contact, source=direct).
2. Bureau: email agent watches the ops inbox (Fixer-style rules or a scheduled Claude skill: "mine the inbox 4×/day, extract to this structure, add to Airtable"). Volume is small (~60 inbound/mo, ~600 emails/mo at Josh scale) — token cost is trivial.
3. Auto-reply fires instantly ("back to you within a couple hours"); team BCC'd. **Human gate:** reply directly, or click to enter the flow. Key-agent list forwards to Ben.

**QUALIFY**
4. AI-assisted prep doc replaces Ben's manual 2-minute review: mission/vision/values (his must-know: does anything connect to investing in the whole person/associate), culture notes, past-speaker budgets if findable, LinkedIn/Crunchbase signals. Delivered to Ben's inbox *before* the call — "a system that would deliver a document to me before the call with all that information would be really helful" is the explicit ask.
5. Date check → **hold created** with stage (1 inquiry / 2 qualified), color-coded, mirrored on calendar. Stale-hold alert replaces memory ("we miss a hold every once in a while" → 0% of the time).

**PITCH / DISCOVERY**
6. Questionnaire form sent ahead where possible; answers land on the deal record so the call double-clicks instead of collects.
7. Call happens (human — this is the sale). Notes field on the record; optional transcript scrape (Fathom) into a notes field — but respect the speaker's style (Josh insisted on his own bullets over AI summaries).
8. Fee stated on call; deal stage and negotiated number updated on the record (list fee vs. negotiated fee vs. final — the "said 40, they had 35" history lives on the deal, not in Ben's head).

**FOLLOW-UP**
9. Next-call-booked? → calendar event linked to deal. If not: automation watches Last Modified + decision-date field → soft check-in template once → hold-release forcing email template.
10. Proposal email assembled from asset library (rate sheet, reel, testimonials, custom paragraph) — recall of IP/assets automated, drafted in Ben's voice, **human clicks send** (no client-facing output auto-sends).

**CLOSE → CASH**
11. Stage → Closed-Won triggers: contract generated (details request + welcome kit go out same moment) → ops review → send → signed → invoice auto-created from contract terms → ops review → send → invoice number lands in QuickBooks.
12. Cash worker matches bank credit (wire/ACH descriptor) to invoice number + amount → proposes "Paid" → **human confirms**. Status chips flow to the deal record and dashboard.
13. Payment plans recorded as structured schedule fields (amount, cadence, #payments) so Liezel never has to ask "what's the amount and how are they paying."

**PRE-EVENT → EVENT**
14. Welcome-kit checklist spawns logistics tasks per event; Sharon/Liezel works the logistics view; Last-Modified staleness report daily ("red alert — 12 days since update").
15. Road-Warrior-style button: speaker clicks one button on the interface the day before → full event brief (his chosen fields only) emailed to him. No logging into Airtable.
16. QR-code flow: checkbox → drop-down qualifier (timeline, event type, own-domain detection e.g. bill@oracle.com → customize) → auto-drafted follow-up ~2 hours later → interested-but-not-now leads onto a value email sequence.

**POST-EVENT**
17. Debrief already on calendar (booked at kickoff). Debrief outcomes logged to the deal: repeat-booking opportunity (spawns next-year deal shell) or feedback data (into client intelligence folder).
18. Journal engine: promo journal send → received? → interested? → bulk order → warehouse (2-day min notice over 200 units; clients want delivery ~1 month out; typical order 100–200) → post-event attendee dropship. Slack channel to warehouse floor remains the human channel.

**CASHFLOW VIEW (the dashboard)**
19. Three columns only: **Owed / Paid / On Hold (weighted)**. Hold stage × fee × {25%, 65%, 100%} = forecast-to-close. Pipeline statuses visible at a glance ("status, status, status — where deals are in a pipe"), fancy dashboards later.

## 2.3 Standing build principles (from all three calls)
- Event is king; contacts link up to it; money links to it.
- Automate the 80–90% that eats time; leave the 6–7 documented edge cases to humans.
- Human-in-the-loop at every irreversible moment: contract send, invoice send, payment confirmed, any client-facing email.
- Don't blow up Liezel's rhythms — mirror calendar + sheets, add grease, don't add 75 steps.
- Extractable data / easy platform exit; backups with version history; modules that fail independently.
- NDAs/sensitive info: handle as per-client exceptions (strip fields on request), not architecture.

# PART 3 — SCRIPT LANGUAGE (Connor's words, recorded)

These are the moments Connor went into actual script. Quotes are lightly cleaned from the transcript but kept in his phrasing — this is the language bank for templates and talk tracks.

### 3.1 Booking the next call before hanging up (~50% hit rate)
Buyer says: *"Three weeks from now we're going to have a conversation with my executive and my boss, and then I'll follow up with you."*

> **"Awesome. Well, let's get a follow-up call scheduled so it's one less thing to worry about. Let's connect on that Friday — how does 4:30 p.m. work for you?"**

Framing when they hesitate:
> **"If we don't need the call — say you book me beforehand — awesome, we can cancel it. Just to have something set in stone. One less thing for you to juggle."**

Positioning the post-decision call (why they'd take it even after deciding):
> **"I value your opinion and what came out of that meeting. I'd love to jump on a call and see how I can help you even more."** *(…"it might be sharing a talk track, it might be a video they requested — instead of burning cycles via email, let's just jump on a call, maybe even five minutes.")*

### 3.2 The forcing email (after one soft check-in goes unanswered)
> **"Hey — are you still considering Ben for this event? If not, please let me know and I'll remove the hold."**

(Connor: this "almost forces the answer." If they're real, they come back with *"whoa, we just need more time"* — to which: *"Awesome. Assuming you're meeting early in the week, how does Friday at 4:30 Eastern work for you?"*)

The one-time soft check-in that precedes it:
> **"Hey, just checking in — have you guys had a chance to talk yet?"** *(use once, then escalate)*

### 3.3 Discovery question ladder (kickoff & pitch calls)
Run as a stated agenda ("we're going to look at content, logistics, some enhancers, and next steps"):
> **"What's the theme of your event? Tell me more about that."**
> **"What's going on in the audience's mind? How are they feeling?"**
> **"Who's the primary stakeholder? So what does this event mean to you? What's your responsibility?"**
> **"What does success of the event look like for you? How does that relate to the organization? How about your team?"**
> **"Does that align with the primary business objectives for the event?"**

Cold-open version (when all you have is company + date):
> **"So we've got this event on October 15th — could you tell me a little more about why you're meeting? What are the objectives?"**
> **"Tell me what success looks like when those leaders walk out the door. Is it more of a culture and networking event to align on a strategic vision, or is this a rally cry to pick ourselves up off the mat and build back better?"**

Warm-open version (when you have context):
> **"Based on what I've read, it looks like you're going through this unbelievable growth trajectory — I'd love to learn more about that."** *(always start by celebrating or checking in if there's an obvious entry point)*

### 3.4 Credibility weaving (structure fixed, content improvised)
Pattern: [their stated pain] → [speaker credibility marker through that lens] → invite the "tell me more":
> **"Because you said your leaders are overwhelmed with AI — well, Josh, being a five-time tech founder investing in emerging companies on the forefront of AI, has learned from founders driving hypergrowth with limited resources and limited time how to actually leverage this in any business."**
> *(They ask how.)* **"Speaking to a dozen founders on a weekly basis, he's learned the five key mindsets for thinking about AI in any organization, big or small."**

Connor's own note on it: *"None of it is a pre-drafted script. The structure always stays the same — I'm aligning the credibility through a unique lens to their pain points or their success points."*

### 3.5 Surfacing budget (the fee drop + pause)
Setup framing:
> **"Just to make sure we drive the most efficient process for us here today, I want to provide you with all the details so that when you get into your committee meeting, you have everything to answer every single question."**

The drop:
> **"When it comes to Josh's investment for his keynote — his keynote fee is $40,000 plus a $3,000 travel buyout. How does that sound to you?"**
> **[PAUSE. Say nothing.]**

Their reaction does the qualifying: *"holy —, that's triple my budget"* / *"that makes sense"* / *"does he discount?"* — "and then you make the call from there."

If they reveal their cap first (best case — rebuttal ready before the fee is even shared):
> Buyer: *"We're capped at a $30,000 budget."*
> **"Well, just to share — Josh's standard rate for an in-person keynote is $40,000 plus a $3,000 travel buyout. But the event's in Florida, and I know he's going to be in Naples in October — I think we can be flexible on that $3,000 travel buyout. Let's set that aside."**

### 3.6 The negotiation close (commit-on-this-call)
> **"Now, when it comes to the $10,000 gap — it's interesting, because when I think of your organization and your challenge, this is exactly the kind of thing Josh admires and loves to immerse himself in. I can feel the entrepreneurial energy."**
> **"I think I could probably get it there — if you can commit on this call to $35,000 flat. And if that's the case, with Josh already in Florida, at $35,000 I think he'd be delighted to work and collaborate with you."**

Then: get the commitment, end the call, **wait a little**, and follow up:
> **"I think we can move forward at 35."**

(Connor's meta-note: the bottom number "is going to be based on what you're comfortable with" — the story you tell about flexibility is constructed live, anchored to something true like existing travel.)

### 3.7 Pre-selling the debrief on the kickoff call
> **"Two weeks after the event — does Monday look good?"**
*(Nimble orgs lock it on the spot; larger ones follow up with times — either way it's scheduled before the event happens, because it was item #1 in the welcome kit they saw before the call.)*

### 3.8 Intake auto-response (bureau/direct inbound)
> **"Thanks so much for the note — we'll be back to you within the next couple of hours."**
*(Instant send, team BCC'd; passes the ball while a human triages.)*

### 3.9 Debrief repeat-booking angle
> **"Let's start thinking about next year. If we talked to your top leaders this year — what about your managers next year?"**
*(Co-create/design next year's event at a strategic level, a year ahead. Relationship + data is the reason to do it every time.)*
